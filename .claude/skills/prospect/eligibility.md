# Advertising eligibility rules

> **Starter rules: edit these to match how you decide.** The researcher reads this file for every business, so a change here applies to the next run.

Give every business exactly one status: **Suitable**, **Review** or **Delete**. Check the rules from top to bottom; the first one that matches wins.

## Delete
- Permanently closed, or no sign of trading in the last 12 months (no recent reviews, posts or website updates, and the website or phone is dead).
- Not a genuine local business: a fake or duplicate listing, a lead-generation listing, or a listing that just forwards to a national call centre.
- A branch of a national or regional chain run by head office (supermarket, bank, big-box retailer, etc.). Local staff can't buy advertising.
- In a category you won't advertise: adult services, gambling, weapons, tobacco/vaping, payday or high-interest lending, debt-management schemes, cannabis/CBD.
- A government body, school or public service.

## Review
- Conflicting or weak evidence (e.g. the website is down but there are recent reviews), or trading status unclear.
- A franchise location (franchisees sometimes buy local advertising, sometimes not).
- A regulated or sensitive category: medical, dental, legal, financial advice, alcohol, pharmacy.
- A charity, church or community group.
- You can't tell what the business actually does, or which area it serves.
- Signs it's for sale, winding down, or under new ownership.

## Suitable
- Independent or locally owned, actively trading (evidence from the last 12 months), clearly legitimate, serving the local area, and not caught by any Delete or Review rule above.

## Writing the justification
Give evidence, not opinions, with dates where you can. For example: "Independent plumber trading since 2015; 14 Google reviews in 2026, website blog updated Oct 2026, Companies House shows the company as active."
